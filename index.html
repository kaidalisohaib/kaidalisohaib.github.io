Mila · McGill Mobile Robotics Lab · May 2026 – now

Can a humanoid balance a free pole on its palm while it walks, using only its head camera?

Yes, in simulation. Pure reinforcement learning never got there, so I put a classical balance controller in the arm and let RL learn the corrections on top.

  • 99.0%success walking, 98.8% standing, over 1,024 test episodes
  • ~30%more accurate pole estimate than a Kalman filter, from a learned estimator
  • 30 / 30walking trials through the robot's real software interface, in a second simulator
  • Isaac Lab
  • PPO
  • LQR
  • DAgger
  • MuJoCo
  • 31 DOF

McGill Mobile Robotics Lab · 2026

Can one neural network walk five different robots?

One policy drives Cassie, Digit, the Unitree G1, H1 and H2, which have between 12 and 31 joints. I added the H2 and fixed the ways it learned to cheat.

  • 5 robotsone PPO policy, with masking for the joints a robot doesn't have
  • 17.5 → 4.0%difference between left and right steps after tuning motor gains from physics
  • 62°forward lean the first H2 policy used as an exploit, removed

Try it

Balance a pole yourself.

This is the textbook version of the H2 problem: a 1.5 m pole on a cart. Move your pointer and the cart follows. With the controller on, it also keeps the pole up. Switch it off and you'll see why the robot needs one.

The controller is LQR, the same kind of balance law that sits inside the H2's arm. It runs in your browser.

About

I've been building robots since I was a kid with a knock-off Mindstorms set, spending hours deciding where the motors should go. In high school I led the software for a competition robot. At McGill I joined the underwater-robot team, and then a research lab that had a humanoid.

What I like most is the part between a controller that works in simulation and one that works on a machine. Most of my time goes into finding out why something that looks fine isn't: a sensor model that was too kind, a test that leaked the answer, a filter that trusted its camera too much.

I try to report numbers the way I'd want to read them: with the number of trials, on data the model hasn't seen, and with the failures left in.

B.Eng. in Computer Engineering, McGill University, expected May 2028. I speak English, French and Arabic.
